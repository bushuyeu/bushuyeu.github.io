Paper review

Reviews and small experiments on papers in robot learning, written for a reading group. Each page ends with a note on how it was produced.

  1. Nov 2026
    DART read-along
    DART, Laskey et al. · arXiv:1703.09327 · read-along
    Add noise to the supervisor's controls while it demonstrates, sized to the learner's error, and get DAgger-like robustness without letting the robot drive. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  2. Nov 2026
    DROID read-along
    DROID, Khazatsky et al. · arXiv:2403.12945 · read-along
    76k robot demonstrations in 564 scenes on a shared Franka hardware design, and what that diversity buys when co-training a policy. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  3. Nov 2026
    Open X-Embodiment read-along
    Open X-Embodiment, Open X-Embodiment Collaboration (O'Neill et al. · arXiv:2310.08864 · read-along
    60 robot datasets from 22 robot types pooled into one format, and what training RT-1 and RT-2 on a 9-robot slice of it buys each robot. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  4. Nov 2026
    Calibrated noise injection for VLA fine-tuning
    DART, Laskey, Lee, Fox, Dragan and Goldberg · arXiv:1703.09327 · visionary
    Does DART's noise, sized to the learner's error, still beat plain isotropic noise when the learner is a pretrained VLA with action chunks? A six-arm test on MimicGen tasks with π0, against MimicGen's own noisy-label default. The application adds the same calibrated pushes to teleoperation, so new fine-tuning data contains recoveries.
  5. Nov 2026
    View, scene and instruction in π0.5-DROID
    DROID, Khazatsky et al. · arXiv:2403.12945 · empiricist
    What does a policy trained on DROID respond to? Offline probes of Physical Intelligence's open π0.5-DROID on 500 DROID states: the other exterior camera changes its actions by 0.15× its own sampling noise, and another lab's exterior image by 1.9×. On 230 states from 46 annotated episodes, another episode's instruction changes them by 0.58×.
  6. Nov 2026
    Robot concentration in Open X-Embodiment
    Open X-Embodiment, Open X-Embodiment Collaboration · arXiv:2310.08864 · empiricist
    How many robots does the Open X-Embodiment data really cover? An audit from public metadata: three robots hold 80% of the trajectories, and the training mixtures of Octo and OpenVLA spread the data across datasets but keep about four effective robots. It also finds that OpenVLA's published mixture table sums to 95%.
  7. Oct 2026
    DAgger read-along
    DAgger, Ross et al. · arXiv:1011.0686 · read-along
    DAgger lets the learner drive, has the expert label the visited states, and retrains on all data; if the expert recovers fast, the T² bound becomes linear in T. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  8. Oct 2026
    OpenVLA read-along
    OpenVLA, Kim et al. · arXiv:2406.09246 · read-along
    A 7B open-source vision-language-action model trained on 970k robot episodes: how it turns actions into tokens, where it beats RT-2-X, and what LoRA and 4-bit inference cost. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  9. Oct 2026
    FAST read-along
    FAST, Pertsch et al. · arXiv:2501.09747 · read-along
    A DCT plus byte-pair-encoding tokenizer that compresses robot action chunks, so next-token VLAs learn 50 Hz dexterous tasks and match diffusion π0 with 5× fewer GPU hours. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  10. Oct 2026
    Octo read-along
    Octo, Ghosh et al. · arXiv:2405.12213 · read-along
    An open transformer policy pretrained on 800k Open X-Embodiment trajectories, which finetunes to new sensors, action spaces and robots from about 100 demonstrations. With the video chapters, prompts for listening in class, a sketchnote to annotate and common questions.
  11. Oct 2026
    Teacher recoverability in DAgger for VLAs
    DAgger, Ross, Gordon and Bagnell · arXiv:1011.0686 · visionary
    Which states should a large VLA teacher label for a small student: the ones it can still recover from, or the ones where the two models disagree? The project compares the two selectors at equal label count and cost, with a recoverability test in simulation. The application sends each failure to the cheapest tested expert that recovers from it: a policy, a planner, then a person.
  12. Oct 2026
    Confidence calibration of fine-tuned OpenVLA
    OpenVLA, Kim, Pertsch, Karamcheti et al. · arXiv:2406.09246 · visionary
    Do the fine-tuning method and the amount of data change how well OpenVLA’s action-token confidence predicts success, and does a calibrator still work after a new fine-tune? Can rollouts in a digital twin set the help threshold of a robot? First results: the released LIBERO-Spatial checkpoint is slightly overconfident (+4.7 points) and barely predicts its failures (AUROC 0.57). On LIBERO-Long it is 36 points overconfident, and its confidence ranks failures below chance (AUROC 0.44).
  13. Oct 2026
    Where FAST came from, and what it led to
    FAST, Physical Intelligence · arXiv:2501.09747 · archeologist
    FAST swapped the per-dimension binning of RT-2 and OpenVLA for DCT and BPE compression. π0.5 and Knowledge Insulation kept FAST tokens for training and added flow matching for inference, while OpenVLA-OFT and BEAST push back on its latency and variable-length tokens. With an interactive citation timeline of 72 references and 140 citing papers.
  14. Sep 2026
    Openpi Comet: a reproduction attempt
    Team Comet, second place in the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge · arXiv:2512.10071 · empiricist
    I priced five claims and ran the two I could afford. The released RFT dataset shows a cap rather than a rebalance and a strong horizon bias, and the released checkpoint scored 0 of 40 on the task the report puts at 1.00.
  15. Sep 2026
    The first-place BEHAVIOR 2025 solution
    Larchenko, Zarin and Karnatak · arXiv:2512.06951 · reviewer
    Honest and reproducible, but correlated-noise flow matching is asserted rather than ablated. Weak reject for a main track, accept as a competition report.
  16. Sep 2026
    What closes SigLIP’s predicate gap on BEHAVIOR?
    Research note with preliminary probe results · empiricist
    72% of BEHAVIOR goal predicates are spatial relations. Linear probes on frozen SigLIP sit at 0.51 to 0.53 on them at the 256-token budget the baselines run, and more tokens do not close the gap.
  17. Feb 2026
    GR00T N1 in context
    NVIDIA · arXiv:2503.14734 · archeologist
    Strongest in data engineering and as the first open humanoid VLA. The dual-system framing is more marketing than architecture, and N1.5 and N1.6 walked away from it within nine months.
  18. Feb 2026
    Cosmos-Predict2.5: world simulation for Physical AI
    NVIDIA · arXiv:2511.00062 · reviewer
    A useful open release judged on its authors’ own benchmark, with a three-trial robotics experiment and no ablation of the recipe. Weak accept.
  19. Feb 2026
    π0.5: a VLA with open-world generalization
    Physical Intelligence · arXiv:2504.16054 · reviewer
    The first VLA shown working in homes it never saw. Accept, with one standard benchmark added and the PaliGemma backbone moved out of the appendix.
  20. Jan 2026
    Does the universal FAST+ tokenizer transfer to cable routing?
    FAST, Physical Intelligence · arXiv:2501.09747 · empiricist
    On Berkeley cable routing, FAST+ keeps a median 81% of a custom-fit tokenizer’s compression, right at my threshold, and the answer turns on how the custom tokenizer is tuned.
