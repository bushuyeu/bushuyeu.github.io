When should a fine-tuned OpenVLA ask for help?

A research project and an application for OpenVLA (Kim, Pertsch, Karamcheti et al., 2024).

Proposed research

Does fine-tuning change calibration?

Monitors depend on one fine-tuned model

Help-request monitors use token confidence directly (Zollo and Zemel), through a classifier (INSIGHT), with conformal prediction (ReconVLA) or through a calibrator (VLAConf). Each monitor is fitted to one fine-tuned model.

The gap

In language models, LoRA on small datasets causes overconfidence, and Bayesian LoRA reduces it. I found no controlled test for VLAs. Zollo and Zemel measured only released OpenVLA checkpoints (LoRA, rank 32).

Proposed research

Hypotheses

  1. H110% of the demonstrations gives a larger confidence-minus-success gap than 100%. At similar success, LoRA gives a larger gap than full fine-tuning.
  2. H2Bayesian LoRA (Laplace-LoRA, 4 adapters) gives a lower calibration error than temperature or action-wise Platt scaling.
  3. H3Under LIBERO-Plus camera, lighting and layout changes, the LoRA-versus-full gap increases with frozen calibrators.

If H1 holds, each new fine-tune needs new labeled rollouts. If H2 holds, a posterior over the adapter repairs the confidence without a new model.

Design

ModelOpenVLA-7B, released pretrained weights.

Data10% (sampled per task) and 100% of the demonstrations.
ConfidenceRaw, temperature, action-wise Platt, Laplace-LoRA, 4-adapter ensemble. One acting adapter.
TasksLIBERO Spatial and Long, then Object and Goal. LIBERO-Plus for H3.
MetricsSuccess, confidence minus success, ECE, Brier, NLL, failure AUROC.
Trials500 per configuration, one seed. 20% / 80% calibration and test splits.

Proposed research

Compute and risks

Compute (estimate)

24 runs on Spatial and Long, 4 of them full fine-tuning. 360–780 A100-hours at OpenVLA’s rates, before Laplace fitting and seeds.

Evaluation needs about 200 GPU-hours. The measured rate is 7 steps per second on one RTX 5090.

Risks

Token-based success confidence is a proxy, and it is weakest late in failed episodes.

About 75 failures in 500 Spatial episodes and 230 in Long. A 20% split holds about 15 Spatial failures. An acting ensemble costs 4× the inference.

Proposed application

Application: rehearsal in a digital twin

  1. Scan the workspace and its objects with 1–3 minutes of phone video.
  2. Rebuild the scene with object meshes, the robot and a matched camera. Set masses and friction by hand, and randomize them.
  3. Rehearse each candidate checkpoint 200–300 times.
  4. Calibrate the confidence and set the help threshold.
  5. Deploy, and compare the failures caught with the prediction of the twin.

Prior work

SIMPLER and ReVeal rank policies in simulated and reconstructed scenes as real ones do. RialTo builds twins from quick scans.

ReVeal does not recover mass or friction. RialTo needs a person to set objects and physics, and it uses point clouds.

Proposed application

Pilot first, then the transfer test

Pilot (go/no-go)

One arm, 2–3 rigid-object tasks, 3 checkpoints × 30 trials in the twin and on the robot. The work continues only if the twin ranks them in the real order and is within ~15 points.

Transfer test

Calibrators from (a) LIBERO, (b) the twin and (c) a few real rollouts are frozen with the twin threshold. Unseen real rollouts then score them.

The measures are the calibration error and, at a fixed help budget, caught failures, false alarms, missed failures and warning time.
