Where FAST came from, and what it led to

FAST: Efficient Action Tokenization for Vision-Language-Action Models. Pertsch, Stachowicz, Ichter, Driess, Nair, Vuong, Mees, Finn and Levine. Physical Intelligence, UC Berkeley and Stanford.

arXiv:2501.09747 · January 2025Pavel Bushuyeu · October 2026bushuyeu.com/review-fast-lineage

The paper

FAST in one slide

The problem

RT-2 and OpenVLA turn each action dimension at each time step into one of 256 bin tokens. At high control rates neighbouring tokens are almost the same, so the model learns to copy the last token.

The fix

Compress each one-second action chunk before tokenizing: a discrete cosine transform per dimension, scale and round as JPEG does, then byte-pair encoding.

π0-FAST matches diffusion π0 with 5× fewer GPU hours of training, but needs about 750 ms per action chunk at inference against about 100 ms (NVIDIA 4090).

How prior work shaped FAST

OpenVLA and RT-2, then π0

Binning, kept and replaced

  • FAST keeps their recipe: a VLM fine-tuned to write action tokens, trained with next-token prediction.
  • Its main baseline is “OpenVLA-style” per-dimension binning (Fig. 2), mostly on the same π0 backbone.
  • Karl Pertsch, FAST’s first author, co-wrote RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo and DROID.

π0: same lab, the base model

π0 is PaliGemma-3B with a flow-matching action expert. π0-FAST keeps the backbone, drops the expert and writes FAST tokens into the VLM’s vocabulary.

The paper compares the two decoders on one model: about 5× less training compute for π0-FAST, about 750 ms against 100 ms per chunk at inference (NVIDIA 4090).

Extends: builds on FAST’s method. Validates: a later system adopts FAST tokens, not an independent test. Related: same problem, not built on FAST.

How later work extends FAST

Knowledge Insulation does FAST's future work

“…testing the combination of compression-based action encodings with non-autoregressive decoding approaches like diffusion…”FAST, discussion of future work
  • Knowledge Insulation (May 2025; Driess, Pertsch, Levine and others) does this in one stage: the VLM learns from FAST tokens, a flow-matching expert produces the actions, and a stop-gradient keeps the expert from damaging the VLM.
  • Train fast like π0-FAST, run fast like π0. π0.5 had used FAST tokens in pre-training and added the expert afterwards; π*0.6 and π0.7 keep the one-stage recipe.

Comparison

What the comparison shows

  • As a tokenizer, FAST is competitive. It beats binning and VQ tokenizers but trails ActionCodec (90.6% vs 95.5%) and BEAST’s parallel variant.
  • Decoding is the common objection. Sequences that do not decode (OAT, ActionCodec), the delay per chunk (OpenVLA-OFT), variable length (BEAST).
  • Training setups differ. The 86.0% vs 71.8% pre-training gap comes from separate reports; FAST reached 90.6% with no robot pre-training (ActionCodec).
  • Its own lab answered the speed objection. π0.5, Knowledge Insulation, π*0.6 and π0.7 train on FAST tokens and act with flow matching.
